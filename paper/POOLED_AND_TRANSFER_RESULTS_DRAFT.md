# Development results for the manuscript

On blocked validation windows from scenarios represented in training, the 192-pixel GPS-plus-image model achieved 79.50% macro success across three seeds. The matched GPS model achieved 78.68%. Success required at least one of five predicted beams to fall within 3 dB of the strongest observed beam. Each scenario received equal weight.

With image dropout and a consistency penalty, pooled success was 79.11%. In leave-one-scenario-out development evaluation, the same regularized model averaged 77.70%, compared with 77.99% for matched GPS and 78.04% for analytical geometry. The largest loss relative to geometry occurred on Scenario 39. A fixed inference rule that retained the three highest-ranked geometry beams and filled two positions from the learned ranking brought transfer success to 77.79% and pooled success to 77.97%. This tradeoff gives little support for using the rule in deployment.

These measurements characterize sensitivity to the evaluation split. The four scenarios were repeatedly explored during model development. Seed averages describe the tested initializations, and uncertainty from scenario sampling remains substantial. The benchmark uses a local blocked forecasting protocol. A publication claim about superiority to an external benchmark requires compatible splits, horizon, inputs and metrics.

Across 16,643 paired validation windows and three seeds, adding images to GPS changed success by +0.431 percentage points in pooled training and -0.286 in transfer. Omitting Scenario 39 reduced the pooled-minus-transfer contrast from 0.717 to 0.040 points. The paired 95% interval was [-0.105,1.208] points when resampling blocks and [0.068,1.540] when resampling recorded sequences. Both estimates condition on four explored scenarios and selected checkpoints.

Within Scenario 39, sequences 50 and 51 together accounted for 77.5% of the net 2.747-point difference between the pooled and transfer visual gains over GPS. Each sequence supplied 187 of the 3,179 validation windows. In sequence 50 the transfer visual model tied geometry on average. In sequence 51 it fell 11.41 percentage points below geometry. The pooled-minus-transfer contrast was +4.343 points within block 39:8 and -0.178 within block 39:9. With only two observed validation blocks, a scenario-wide explanation for the visual loss remains uncertain.

Within each scenario the train and validation partitions shared zero sequence identifiers, recorded blocks, input/target raw rows, and front or rear input-image paths. On the recorded CSV clocks, the closest train and validation rows were separated by 20.10, 20.10, 20.10, 395.31 seconds for Scenarios 36–39 respectively. This audit verifies identity separation and chronology. Different sequences can still contain similar roads or scenes.

Across the four excluded scenarios and three seeds, endpoint differences gave 77.89% top-five success, compared with 77.99% for matched GPS and 78.04% for geometry. Mean received-power loss was 4.965 dB for endpoint differences, 5.015 dB for GPS and 5.017 dB for geometry. The paired endpoint-minus-geometry difference was -0.052 dB (95% block interval -0.093 to +0.009). The similar rates of losses above 10 dB (16.79% and 16.73%, respectively) give little evidence of improved tail performance. Power-loss analysis was exploratory.

Across the four transfer folds and three seeds, every beam in the geometry top 20 lost more than 10 dB on 14.23% of validation windows (equal scenario weight). The robust mean-feature model lost more than 10 dB on 16.80% of windows. Candidate exclusion therefore accounted for 84.7% of its severe misses, with 2.57 percentage points of windows attributable to selecting five beams within the shortlist. At the primary 3 dB threshold, candidate exclusion accounted for 68.4% of misses. These are conditional oracle diagnostics using saved validation powers.

With seed 42, widening the geometry shortlist from 20 to 40 changed mean transfer success for the visual model by -0.017 percentage points (paired block interval -0.065 to +0.030). On Scenario 39, mean loss fell by 0.575 dB. Holding the K40 model scores fixed and restricting its choices back to K20 attributed 0.327 dB to access to the additional beams and 0.248 dB to changed ranking within the original shortlist. The visual model selected a newly eligible beam on 826 of 3,179 windows there, with 519 improved, 285 worsened, and 22 unchanged relative to the restricted K40 ranking. It selected no newly eligible beam on the other three folds. This is an exploratory single-seed finding.

Across three seeds and four transfer scenarios, increasing the visual model's shortlist from 20 to 40 raised macro top-five success within 3 dB by 0.329 percentage points (paired block interval -0.106 to +0.576). Scenario 39 was strongly seed-dependent. Success changed by -0.03, 0.00 and +4.09 points for seeds 42, 7 and 13, respectively. The corresponding fractions of windows where a newly eligible beam entered the final five were 25.98%, 0.00% and 1.51%.

At width 40, adding front and rear RGB to the matched GPS model changed success by +0.011 points (interval -0.007 to +0.037) and mean power loss by -0.009 dB (interval -0.022 to +0.010). Against analytic geometry the visual model changed success by -0.007 points and mean loss by -0.053 dB. These exploratory transfer results support reporting a small mean power-loss difference against geometry. They do not establish an incremental RGB benefit across scenarios. The prespecified one-point development threshold was missed.

## Full-codebook comparison

We evaluated a paper-based shifted-window adaptation with all 256 beams available and paired each RGB-plus-GPS fit with a GPS-only fit. Across 30 fits, the pooled-minus-transfer change in the incremental camera contribution to top-five success within 3 dB was -1.39 percentage points (95% paired block interval -13.01 to +8.49). This comparison did not independently confirm the direction of the earlier split effect. Both architectures were evaluated on the same four previously explored scenarios.

In excluded-scenario validation, exact-best-beam top-five accuracy averaged 29.04% with RGB and 23.61% with GPS. The paired difference was +5.42 points (interval -2.86 to +9.98). Within-3-dB success averaged 48.71% with RGB, 68.56% with GPS and 78.04% with analytic geometry. The paired RGB-minus-GPS difference was -19.86 points (interval -26.30 to -11.26). A check against the saved power profiles found that every reference label lay within 3 dB of the maximum. Consequently, the fraction of windows that succeeded through an alternative beam without an exact hit was 19.67% with RGB and 44.95% with GPS. This accounts arithmetically for the divergence between metrics. It does not identify its training cause. The findings apply to this adaptation and its selected checkpoints.
